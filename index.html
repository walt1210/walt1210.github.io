Thesis

MAIze: offline maize disease diagnosis with retrieval-grounded guidance

Undergraduate thesis, Angeles University Foundation. A four-person team. My part: I built the retrieval-augmented guidance backend, helped develop and improve the model pipeline, and worked on parts of the Android app.

Maize streak virus (MSV) and maize lethal necrosis (MLN) are hard to tell apart in the field, and a reliable mobile signal is not guaranteed on many farms. Our Android app takes one photo of a leaf and, entirely on the phone, checks that the photo shows a maize leaf, classifies it as healthy, MSV, or MLN, outlines the symptoms, and estimates severity. When a connection is available, the app can also request written management guidance from a cloud backend.

The vision model

The final model is a MobileNetV3-Small network with three output heads, trained on pseudo-labels produced by larger teacher models and checked against 501 hand-annotated images. I contributed to building and improving this pipeline, and I was one of the two severity raters.

Speed and sizeAbout 60 milliseconds per image on a CPU, in a 13.75 MB TensorFlow Lite file, with 100 percent agreement between the original and converted model
SeveritySpearman correlation of 0.875 with the consensus of two raters from our team, including me, who scored independently and agreed with each other at a Cohen's Kappa of 0.91
SegmentationLeaf outline IoU of 0.75 and symptom IoU of 0.45 against human annotations
Classification64.6 percent accuracy on 27,228 held-out test images

The classification number is the weak point, and the confusion matrix explains why. The model detects MLN well (92 percent recall) but over-predicts it, labeling many healthy leaves as MLN (healthy recall of only 32 percent). We inspected the misclassified healthy images by hand and confirmed the labels were correct, so the problem is a real model bias, likely from glare and natural discoloration. Rebalancing the training data is the first item in our future work.

The guidance backend

I built the retrieval-augmented guidance service: a Flask API on Google Cloud Run that retrieves passages from a curated knowledge base in ChromaDB, asks Gemini for structured advice, validates the response against a schema, and falls back to prepared guidance when generation fails.

A plant pathology expert rated 30 guidance cases at 4.67 out of 5 overall, but also marked 23 of them for revision. The main problems were pesticide rates carried over from foreign sources and destructive measures suggested before official confirmation. Those findings became concrete rules for what the guidance must say before any public release. A separate language review found the Tagalog responses much weaker than the English ones (2.25 versus 3.83 out of 5), so they need full human revision.

Built with PyTorch, TensorFlow Lite, ONNX, Grad-CAM++, Kotlin, Python, Flask, Gemini API, ChromaDB, RAGAS, Docker, Google Cloud Run

Closed-loop text-to-SQL agent

One Gemini model writes a SQL query from a plain-English question, and a second model reviews it against the live database schema before it runs. If the query still fails, the agent diagnoses the error, writes a simpler fallback query, and returns a result clearly marked as partial instead of crashing.

Two-agent research pipeline

A CrewAI pipeline where one agent plans research with web search and a second writes a report using a calculator tool. The report looked polished, but the execution trace showed that both agents mostly skipped their tools, and the writer invented a revenue figure that was wrong in both runs. Reading the trace, not the final report, is what caught it.
